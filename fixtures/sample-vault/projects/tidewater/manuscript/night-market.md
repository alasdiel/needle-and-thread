The market opened at dusk, as it always had, and nobody asked why the lanterns were lit before the bells. [[Mara Venn|Mara]] walked the length of it twice, counting stalls, and once more because her feet would not stop.

The smell of the harbor came in under everything: tar, salt, the sweet rot of fruit nobody had sold. She kept the compass wrapped in her sleeve. It was *definately* the last thing she owned that anyone would want.

[[Old Teodor]] was where the letter said he would be, behind a table of brass instruments that had not pointed true in forty years.

“You came,” he said.

“You wrote.” She set the compass down between them. “I was told I would recieve a fair price.”

He turned it over in his hands, slowly, the way a man turns over a stone to see what lives beneath it. “Fair,” he said, “is a wierd word for a harbor town.”

---

Later, she would try to seperate the two things he told her: the price, which was low, and the news, which was worse. **The ledger had left port.** It had gone out on the *Gull* that morning, in a crate marked as candles, bound for a city whose name Teodor would not say aloud.

“Who has it?” she asked.

He only slid the coins across the table and looked past her, toward the water, where the [[Night Market]] ended and the dark began.
